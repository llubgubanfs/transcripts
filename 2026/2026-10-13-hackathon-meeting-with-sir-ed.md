# Hackathon meeting with Sir Ed

## Executive summary

**TL;DR**
- Harvey's team pitched "Relay Launch" to Sir Ed and Maricel: a 2-day, 9–7 event where participants compete only within their own role (PM, UI/UX, Dev, QA), and only the winning output of each stage passes to the next [00:19–04:26].
- Sir Ed had no problem with the setup. His open questions: enough UI/UX and PM participants, judge availability, what the participants build, and how to score fairly [20:30].
- Nothing is decided. Everything was input, and the team will bring a revised draft back for the next review [43:06].

**Proposal as presented [01:48–07:27]**
- Flow: client brief → PM product spec → UI/UX design → Dev build → QA. Each role starts from the same official input (the previous stage's winning output).
- Individual competition, not teams. One dedicated judge (or panel) per role, with a 30-minute judging gate after each stage.
- Draft timetable: PM 9:30–12:00, UI/UX 2:00–5:30, Dev about 9:00–3:00 on day 2, QA 1.5 hours. All timings are non-final.

**Feedback and ideas from Sir Ed and Vic**
- Participant supply: Dev and QA are plentiful, but PM and UI/UX may be a "couple" at most. Check headcount and schedules via Rocks [07:29–09:29]. Maricel noted UI/UX is few, maybe one [29:49].
- Judges: realistically 1–2 per role, and some would rather compete than judge [11:23]. Suggested 8-person team as panels plus one outside expert per role, with weighted scores (e.g. 25% / 50%) [19:46–27:52].
- Brief and rules: decide what participants build, whether tokens or models are capped (e.g. a single model for fairness), and keep scope bounded (about 5 major functions) so scoring is comparable [12:33–14:01, 41:36].
- Schedule: weekday (Thu/Fri), not weekend. Night-shift people need start times of about 6–7pm. Alternative: a fixed 3–6pm slot per role, one role per day over about 4 days [14:23–16:58].
- Why 2 days: 24 hours was too much given client work, and a 1-week window was considered but rejected. Sir Ed floated an "amazing race" idea: each participant clocks 3 hours from the moment they receive the brief, anytime that week, with a timestamp-based deduction if over time [17:19–19:34, 35:06].
- Scoring: use AI-assisted rubrics but keep humans in the loop; judge on quality, complexity, error handling, security, unit and regression tests, and not recycled apps [20:43–26:03]. Vic's criteria suggestions: business design, complexity, error handling. QA is hard to judge; ideas include bugs found and test-case coverage [40:00–41:25].
- Participation: level 3 and up were unparticipative in training. A clear reward should motivate them. Messaging should be "open to all" with no categories, rather than pushing the bench [30:48–35:06].
- Giveaways and winners are a separate budget question for Sir Ed and Lloyd [28:31].

**Action items (Harvey's team)**
- [ ] Harvey's team: check Rocks for possible participants and judges, and their schedules [26:27–26:53]
- [ ] Harvey's team: choose the schedule option (per-day 3–6pm vs. the drafted 2-day plan) based on those numbers [26:53]
- [ ] Harvey's team: decide what the participants will build and the client brief [27:08]
- [ ] Harvey's team: draft the scoring and who scores each role, as a weighted table with experts [27:13–27:52]
- [ ] Harvey's team: plan the winners' budget, judge giveaways and marketing materials [28:31–29:06]
- [ ] Harvey's team: ping Sir Ed to schedule the next review once the next version is drafted [44:00]

**Open questions**
- How many PM and UI/UX participants and judges are actually available?
- Fixed 2-day window vs. per-role 3-hour slots vs. "amazing race" timing?
- Is one model or token cap enforced for AI use?
- Winner count (Sir Ed said "4 winners", one per role) and budget [28:31].
- Transcript names (Maricel, Vic, Lloyd) are Gemini's spellings and some garbled words in the scoring discussion are uncertain.
